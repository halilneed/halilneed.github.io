Turkish prompt injection classifier

turkish-prompt-injection-classifier labels Turkish text going into an LLM as BENIGN or INJECTION: user messages, and the content an assistant reads, such as emails, documents, retrieved passages and tool output.

What it is good at: not blocking ordinary Turkish. On 2,875 benign Turkish business texts and task instructions it flags 0 to 1.1%. The three public guards I compared flag up to 77% of the same task instructions. What it is not good at yet: attacks from a source it has never seen. There it is behind two multilingual guards.

Results

There are three Turkish training sources. The honest number is source held out: for each test set, a separate model is trained without any row from that source and tested on that source’s test split. The released model is trained on all three; on their test splits it is in-distribution and scores higher than you should expect on your own traffic. I ran every model with the same script and a threshold of 0.5. These are my measurements, not the authors’ published numbers.

The three sets are my KVKK benchmark, my BSEBY benchmark and the instructions of cagrigungor’s PII benchmark, all CC0. Where it fails

  • Encoded attacks (base64, Caesar, reversed text): 5 of 13 in fevzi. proventra catches 10, wolf-defender 12.
  • Turkish morphological rewording (bureaucratic paraphrase, suffix games): 9 of 15 in fevzi.
  • Agentic attacks from an unseen source (conv, source held out): indirect content injection 0 of 3, data exfiltration 0 of 3, tool abuse 1 of 3, memory poisoning 1 of 3. Three rows each, so read them as a direction.
  • The deepset translation style (berat, source held out): recall 0.170.

Where it holds up: users changing their own earlier instruction. In the 1k test set, 6 of 7 with that source held out and 7 of 7 with all sources; proventra gets 0 of 7 and wolf-defender 1 of 7.

A lower threshold trades false alarms for recall. At 0.25 the source-held-out conv F1 goes from 0.588 to 0.679 and the banking-text false-positive rate from 1.1% to 1.3%. I picked that value after seeing the test numbers, so treat it as a direction, not a tuned setting.

Python quick start

from transformers import pipeline

clf = pipeline("text-classification", model="halilneed/turkish-prompt-injection-classifier")
print(clf("Önceki mesajımdaki formatı unut, bu sefer tablo olarak yaz."))
# [{'label': 'BENIGN', 'score': 0.988}]

Measured speed: 22.9 ms per text on average, 36.8 ms at the 95th percentile, at batch size one on an Intel i5-12400F with eight threads, for texts of 119 characters on average. Text beyond 512 tokens is cut off, so classify long documents in chunks and flag the document if any chunk is INJECTION.

This is one layer. Keep an agent’s tools on least privilege, ask for confirmation before irreversible actions, and treat retrieved content as data whatever the model says.

Training data

No new attack text was written for this model. Attacks come only from public Turkish datasets; the benign side adds Turkish text I already had.

  • 3nesdeniz/turkish-conversation-prompt-injection (CC-BY-4.0): train and validation, 120 attacks and 510 benign rows.
  • 3nesdeniz/turkish-prompt-injection-1k (CC-BY-4.0): train and validation, 471 attacks and 416 benign rows.
  • beratcmn/turkish-prompt-injections (Apache-2.0): train, 199 attacks and 300 benign rows.
  • My own benign text: 2,998 synthetic business texts from the training splits of my KVKK and BSEBY classifiers, and 1,476 masking instructions and instruction-plus-text pairs from the training split of my PII model.
  • Method. Full fine-tune of BERTurk, 4 epochs, learning rate 3e-5, bfloat16, up to 384 tokens. Attack rows repeated twice, and a share of rows added without Turkish characters and in lower case, because people type that way. The checkpoint was chosen on a 10% development split of the training mix (dev F1 0.956). Test splits were never used for training, model selection or the threshold.

Limitations

  • The test sets are small: 30, 79 and 59 attacks. One or two rows move F1 by several points.
  • All Turkish sources are synthetic or translated. None is a sample of real production traffic.
  • The model reads one piece of text at a time. It does not see the conversation, the system prompt or which tool the text came from.
  • It is not a content moderation model. A harmful request without any manipulation is BENIGN by design.
  • Not compared: Llama Prompt Guard 2 (gated, no access) and a scikit-learn Turkish detector that does not load in transformers.

Türkçe prompt injection sınıflandırıcısı

turkish-prompt-injection-classifier, bir LLM’e giren Türkçe metni BENIGN (zararsız) ya da INJECTION olarak işaretleyen küçük bir sınıflandırıcıdır. Kullanıcı mesajlarında ve asistanın okuduğu içerikte çalışır: e-posta, belge, getirilen pasaj, araç çıktısı.

Eğitimde görmediği 2.875 zararsız Türkçe metinde yanlış alarm oranı %0 ile %1,1 arasındadır; karşılaştırdığım üç açık model aynı görev talimatlarının %77’sine kadarını saldırı sayıyor. Görmediği bir kaynaktan gelen saldırılarda ise iki çok dilli modelin gerisindedir. Tek başına bir savunma değildir.
